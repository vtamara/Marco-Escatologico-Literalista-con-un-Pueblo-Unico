The brief document ([`README.md`](README.md)) presents the core: the one people

## 4. Eschatological proposal

### 4.1 Central interpretive line

**Historical premillennialism with a pretribulational rapture, without dispensationalism.**

Christ will return before the literal 1000-year millennium. The Church will be
caught up before the tribulation in order to be delivered from the wrath to
come. The dispensational separation between Israel and the Church is rejected:
Israel and the Church are one single people of God, defined by faith in Christ,
not by genetics nor by Zionist agendas.

### 4.2.1 How to read the "Date" column

The table mixes two different scales, and they should not be confused.

**Absolute scale (stages 1-14).** These are calendar dates, with five levels of
certainty:

| Level | What it means | Stages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Precise** | Date verifiable from an external historical source | 10 (chronology of the Kings, anchored in Qarqar, 853 BC); 14 (destruction of Jerusalem, AD 70) |
| **Approximate** | Standard estimate in the literature, without an exact year | 5, 6, 9, 11, 13 |
| **Debated** | Two or more legitimate options exist, and both are presented | 7 (Exodus 1446 or 1277 BC) |
| **Derived** | Depends on a debated stage | 8 (follows the option chosen in 7) |
| **Blank** | No consensus and no basis for calculation | 1, 2, 3, 4 |

**Relative scale (stages 15-23).** These are not dates but positions relative to
a point that only God knows:

- **R** = pretribulational rapture (1 Thess 4:13-18; 1 Cor 15:51-52). Its date is indeterminate by definition (Mt 24:36; Acts 1:7).
- **R+3.5** = midpoint of the seven-year week of Dan 9:27 (Mt 24:15; Rev 13:5: forty-two months).
- **R+7** = end of the tribulation and second coming (Rev 19:11-21; Mt 24:29-31; Zech 14:1-9).
- **R+7 to R+1007** = 1000-year millennium (Rev 20:4-6).
- **R+1007** = release of Satan, final rebellion and judgment (Rev 20:7-15).

**What the table is not.** It is not a calendar nor a dated prophecy: it is a
**sequence of dependencies**. What the table asserts is the *order* of the events
and their textual anchor, not the time intervals between them beyond those the
text itself declares (seven years, forty-two months, a thousand years).

### 4.2.2 The thread of the timeline in five spans

The table of §4.2 is best read as a narrative with five spans.

**Span 1 — Eternal prologue (stages 1-4).** Original rebellion, the fall of
humanity and the covenants with Adam (Gen 3:15, the first promise of the
Restorer) and with Noah (Gen 9:8-17, the promise that history will not be
repeated as a total judgment before the end). These stages establish the
**problem** that the rest of the line resolves: there is a defeated enemy, a
fallen humanity and a promise of redemption. Their dates remain blank and are not
used as an argument.

**Span 2 — Formation of the people by faith (stages 5-9).** The covenant with
Abraham (Gen 12:1-3; 15; 17) opens the promise to "all the families of the
earth"; Jacob receives the name Israel (Gen 32:24-28; Hos 12:3-4); the Exodus
incorporates the mixed multitude and the resident foreigner (Ex 12:38, 48-49);
the conquest incorporates Rahab and Caleb; Ruth the Moabite enters despite the
prohibition of Deut 23:3. This span is the one that sustains §2 of the README:
**belonging is defined by faith, not by genealogy**, and the text shows it before
Christ, not only after.

**Span 3 — Davidic covenant and Messiah (stages 10-13).** The covenant with David
(2 Sam 7:12-16; Ps 89:3-4) fixes the expectation of the eternal Son; the birth
(Gal 4:4-5), the declaration of Jesus (Lk 10:18) and the cross (Col 2:15;
Jn 12:31-33; Heb 2:14) fulfil the promise and defeat the enemy of span 1. The
cross is the point where the "problem" of the prologue is resolved in principle.

**Span 4 — The hinge of AD 70 (stage 14).** The destruction of the temple
(Mt 24:1-2; Mk 13:1-2; Lk 21:5-6; Heb 8:13) closes the Mosaic covenant as the
centre of worship and opens the age of the church. Without this span, the table
would have no reason to change scale: it is the point where prophecy moves from
"history of Israel" to "history of the one people".

**Span 5 — The consummation (stages 15-23).** Rapture (15, with the final
expulsion from heaven and the sealing of the 144,000 in 16-17), the seven-year
tribulation with its midpoint (18), the second coming (19), the imprisonment of
Satan (20), the thousand-year millennium (21), the final rebellion (22) and
eternal consignment (23). The detail of each event is in §4.3-§4.5 and in the
fitting of prophecies in §5.

**How stages 19-22 chain together.** They are not a single leap. The second
coming (19) and the imprisonment of Satan (20) share the mark R+7 because the
text presents them together (Rev 19:11-21 and 20:1-3). From there the millennium
**adds** its thousand years: if it begins at R+7, it ends at R+7+1000, that is,
R+1007. That is why stage 22 carries R+1007 and not R+7: the final rebellion
occurs at the **end** of the millennium, not at its beginning (Rev 20:7-9).

### 4.2.4 Open tension of the timeline

Every timeline that runs from creation to the eternal state has points where the
text is silent. They are declared:

1. **Stages 1-4 have no date and cannot be used as an argument.** That Gen 1-11 is real history does not imply that its chronology is calculable. The table includes them for narrative completeness, not as evidence.
2. **The 1000-year millennium depends on reading Rev 20:2-7 literally.** That is precisely the methodological commitment of the framework (the literalist method), not an independent exegetical conclusion. Whoever reads the number as symbolic does not break the one people; he only changes the temporal scale of span 5.
3. **The relation between Lk 10:18, Col 2:15 and Rev 12:7-9 is not dated by the text.** The framework proposes a progression of defeats (cf. §5.8, tension 6), but acknowledges that it is harmonisation, not direct exegesis.
4. **Stages 15-17 are grouped under R without the text indicating their internal order.** 1 Thess 4:13-18 says nothing about the 144,000 nor about Rev 12; placing them at the same point is inference.
5. **The end of the tribulation and the second coming are treated as a single event (stage 19).** If it were shown that the text separates them, the table would need one more stage; today it does not have it.

| Phase | Participants | Key text | Purpose |

| **1. Rapture** | The dead and the Church of the present age | 1 Thess 4:16; 1 Cor 15:23 | Judgment seat of Christ and gathering |

**Note**: The framework holds that the Old Testament saints are possibly
resurrected at the final judgment (Phase 3), not at the start of the millennium.
It does not assert this as demonstrated; it declares it as theological inference
(see §6).

### 4.4 The millennium: imposed justice, not consummated perfection

The millennium is not the eternal state. There is death and potential sin
(Isa 65:20-25). The structure is:
- **Resurrected immortals** (martyrs of the tribulation + raptured believers) who rule with Christ.
- **Natural mortal survivors** (Mt 25:31-46) who are governed, multiply, and can still sin and die.

**Why it is necessary**: If everyone in the millennium were resurrected
immortals, there would be no need to "rule with a rod of iron" (Rev 2:27; 19:15),
nor would there be a rebellion at the end (Rev 20:7-9). Rebellion is possible
because there are unregenerate mortals who, like the fallen angels, resist the
evidence and the perfect government.

The millennium is Christ's reign over all nations, not an era of Jewish ethnic
dominion. Christ is the temple (Rev 21:22), and there is no third physical
temple. This refutes the dispensationalist version of the millennium as a
restoration of the temple and the law.

### 4.6 Falsification criterion for this chapter

If it is shown that the Old Testament saints are resurrected at the rapture or at
the second coming, or that the millennium is an era of Jewish ethnic dominion,
this chapter falls.

### 4.7 The conclusion in its developed form

The README closes with a brief conclusion. This is its extended version, with the
chains of texts that are only cited there.

**The thesis, with its chain of texts.** There is one single people of God, and
its criterion is faith in Christ, not genealogy nor the sovereignty of a State
(Gal 6:16; Rom 9:6-8; Rom 11:17-24). That definition does not begin in the New
Testament:

- The name "Israel" is received through wrestling and pleading, not through inheritance (Gen 32:24-28; Gen 35:10; Hos 12:3-4).
- Already in the Old Testament there entered by faith circumcised foreigners (Gen 17:12-13), the mixed multitude (Ex 12:38), the resident foreigner (Ex 12:48-49), Rahab (Josh 2; Mt 1:5), Caleb (Num 32:12) and Ruth (Ruth 1:16-17; Mt 1:5), the latter despite the law excluding her (Deut 23:3).
- The Northern Israel kept the name without the faith and was not the Israel of God (1 Kings 12:26-33; Hos 12:3-4; Isa 48:1-2).
- The New Testament applies to the one people the titles of Israel (1 Pet 2:9-10, from Ex 19:5-6 and Isa 43:20-21) and declares that Christ made both peoples one (Eph 2:14-16).

**The consequence, stage by stage.** If the people is one, every piece of the
dispensationalist scheme loses its basis:

| Dispensationalist piece | What sustains it | Why it falls |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| The Church is a parenthesis | Two peoples with two plans | There are not two peoples (Gal 6:16; Rom 11:17-24) |
| Israel's clock stopped at AD 70 | Postponement of the kingdom | AD 70 closes the Mosaic covenant (Mt 24:1-2; Heb 8:13); it does not postpone it |
| Third temple in the tribulation and the millennium | Restoration of Levitical worship | Christ is the temple (Jn 2:19-21; Rev 21:22) |
| Millennium of ethnic dominion | Israel as a permanent subject | The millennium is Christ's reign over the nations (Rev 20:4-6; Isa 65:20-25) |
| Old Testament saints resurrected at the start of the millennium | Reading of Dan 12:2 | They are resurrected at the final judgment (inference; Rev 20:11-15) |

What remains is the literalist method applied coherently, without the hypothesis
of the two peoples: pretribulational rapture (1 Thess 4:13-18), seven-year
tribulation (Dan 9:27; Mt 24:15-22), literal 1000-year millennium (Rev 20:4-6)
and second coming at the end of the tribulation (Rev 19:11-21).

**The application, with its limits.** The distinction between biblical Israel and
the modern State is not a polemical addition: it follows from the thesis. Its
limits are those of §8.2: Israel is not denied the right to exist, nor are Jews
denied the right to live in the land; the divine function that Christian Zionism
attributes to it is denied, and the same measure is asked for everyone. The full
ethical warning is in §8.5: a theology that leads to dehumanising brothers in the
faith cannot come from the wisdom from above (Jas 3:17; Gal 6:10).

**What is not claimed.** This is not the only possible reading. The
falsification criteria are in §4.6, §6 and in the corresponding chapters of the
README (§2.6, §3.6). The fruit-based argument does not sustain the thesis: it
confirms and warns (§8.1).

## 5. Fitting of some prophecies into the eschatological proposal

### 5.1 Criteria for not forcing the scheme

| Criterion | Description |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The text must indicate the division** | If the passage itself says "first... then", "midpoint of the week", "time, times and half a time", or distinguishes stages, it may be divided. If not, it may not. |
| **The New Testament must authorise the reinterpretation** | If an Old Testament prophet is cited by Jesus or the apostles with a partial fulfilment, it may be harmonised. If there is no citation, caution is required. |
| **What the text unites may not be separated** | For example, the coming of the Messiah may not be separated from his reign if the text presents them as a single event, unless the New Testament does so. |
| **The structure must be coherent with the literary genre** | Prophetic poetry can have progressive fulfilment; historical narrative cannot. |

### 5.3 The woman of Revelation 12

The woman of Rev 12:1-2 is the **people of God**, the same Israel after Jacob's
change of name, who gives birth to the Messiah (Rev 12:5) and then flees to the
desert (Rev 12:6, 14).

| Element | Interpretation |
| :--- | :--- |
| **The woman** | The people of God (biblical Israel and the Church), who gives birth to the Messiah. She includes Mary as the historical mother, but is not exhausted in her. |
| **The male child** | Jesus Christ (Rev 12:5), caught up to the throne (the ascension). |
| **The flight to the desert (1260 days)** | The faithful remnant (Jews and Gentiles converted during the tribulation) is supernaturally protected by God in a prepared place (Rev 12:6, 14). |
| **The rest of her offspring (Rev 12:17)** | The believers of the second half of the tribulation, who keep the commandments and have the testimony of Jesus. |

**Reference to §4.2, stages 11, 15, 16, 18.**

| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |

| **Tartarus** | Prison in darkness for the angels who sinned, reserved until the judgment. It is neither Hades nor the lake of fire. | Biblical text | 2 Pet 2:4 (`ταρταρώσας`, `σειραῖς ζόφου`, `εἰς κρίσιν τετηρημένους`); Jude 6 (`δεσμοῖς ἀϊδίοις ὑπὸ ζόφον`) |
| **Identification of those angels with those of Gen 6:2-4** | The angels who sinned would be those who abandoned their own domain (Jude 6) in the days of Noah. | Theological inference | Gen 6:1-4; Jude 6; 2 Pet 2:4-6 |
| **Death** | Power/consequence of sin that will be abolished as the last enemy. | Biblical text | 1 Cor 15:26; Rev 20:14 |
| **Lake of fire** | Final destination of the wicked, of Satan, of death and of Hades. It is the "second death". | Biblical text | Rev 20:10, 14-15; 21:8 |

**What the text does not say:** neither the number of angels, nor the date of the
confinement, nor the location of Tartarus. `ταρταρώσας` is a *hapax legomenon* of
the New Testament.

**Distinction from Rev 12:7-9:** the text presents those of Tartarus as confined
("reserved for judgment") and those of Rev 12 as thrown down to the earth. They
are different destinies, and the text does not give the chronology that
reconciles them (cf. §5.8, tension 6).

**Reference to §4.2, stage 23.**

### 5.8 Open tensions

1. **Imminence of the rapture vs. general signs (Mt 24:4-14)**: The general signs are fulfilled throughout the age and prepare the scenario of the antichrist, but they do not condition the rapture. Global preaching (Mt 24:14) is the clearest sign of our time, fulfilled by the Church before the rapture. How to harmonise this with absolute imminence remains open.
2. **Matthew 24:36-44: rapture or second coming?**: The text allows at least two readings. The current reading (rapture) implies a possible chronological inversion in Jesus' discourse. The text is not forced; the tension is acknowledged.
3. **Luke 18:8 ("Will he find faith on the earth?")**: It underlines the scarcity of the remnant, not its total absence (cf. Mt 25:31-46).
4. **The 144,000**: They are believers of the tribulation, sealed for their special faith, not for genetics.
5. **False christs in the second half (Mt 24:23-26)**: They coexist with the antichrist's dominion and persecution unto death; they are two forms of deception in the same period of chaos.
6. **Expulsion of Satan (Rev 12:7-9)**: A future eschatological event (R), distinct from the original rebellion, with a progression of defeats (Lk 10:18; Col 2:15; Rev 12:7-9; Rev 20:10).
   - **Biblical text**: there is war in heaven, and the dragon with his angels is thrown down to the earth (`ἐβλήθη εἰς τὴν γῆν ... καὶ οἱ ἄγγελοι αὐτοῦ ἐβλήθησαν μετ' αὐτοῦ`, Rev 12:9). That it is future is the framework's reading (R).
   - **Distinction from Tartarus (§5.7)**: those of 2 Pet 2:4 and Jude 6 are confined "reserved for judgment" (`εἰς κρίσιν τετηρημένους`), while those of Rev 12 are thrown down to the earth. The text does not give the chronology that reconciles both passages.
7. **The temple of Ezekiel 40-48**: A typological vision that points to Christ and to the new creation (Rev 21:22), not a literal blueprint for the millennium.
8. **Testimonies of angels in modern wars**: Accounts circulate of soldiers who claim to have seen angels fighting alongside Israel in 1948, 1967 and 1973.
   - **Biblical text**: Satan disguises himself as an angel of light (2 Cor 11:14), and the criterion of a divine mission was never the sign but the verifiable prophetic command (Deut 13:1-3; 18:21-22). After Christ, every spirit must confess Jesus come in the flesh (1 Jn 4:1-3).
   - **Inference**: the text does not permit identifying spiritual manifestations in modern conflicts, neither for nor against. To affirm that they were angels of God, or that they were fallen angels, is in both cases an inference about which Scripture is silent.
   - **State of the evidence**: the accounts are second or third hand, without identifiable witnesses and without the backing of military archives; the sources are mostly devotional. The best-studied analogous case, the "Angels of Mons" (1914), turned out to originate in a fiction story by Arthur Machen that was taken for real testimony (Clarke, *Rumours of Angels*, Folklore 2004).
   - **Documented historical context**: psychological warfare has used supernatural fear as a weapon. Verifiable cases: the US *Wandering Soul* operation in Vietnam (recordings simulating spirit voices to frighten the adversary) and the World War II *Ghost Army* programme (decoys and acoustic deception). They are cited only as **documented precedents that accounts in combat can be induced or amplified**, not as an explanation of the Israeli case, for which there is no evidence.
   - **Methodological caution**: it is not affirmed or insinuated that these accounts come from deliberate manipulation, nor that there is infiltration of agendas in specific churches or denominations. Such claims would require verifiable primary sources, and the framework does not have them. The document's argument does not depend on them: it is enough to show that the **criterion** for validating a divine mission is prophetic and biblical, not experiential.
   - **Use in this framework**: it is not used as an argument. The criterion remains prophetic and biblical (cf. README §3.3 and §8.3).

## 6. Falsification criteria of the complete framework

1. **One people (Church / ethnic Israel distinction)**: If it is shown that the Church is not the Israel of God (Gal 6:16) or that the olive tree is not one (Rom 11:17-24), the framework falls. This is the principal criterion: it attacks the foundation of dispensationalism.
2. **Resurrection of the Old Testament saints**: If it is shown exegetically that they are resurrected at the rapture or at the second coming, the three-phase thesis falls.
3. **Millennium**: If it is shown that the millennium is an era of Jewish ethnic dominion with restoration of the temple and the law, the framework falls.
4. **Cessation of the temple**: If it is shown that the physical temple remains the centre of the collective covenant with Israel after Christ, the cessation thesis falls.
5. **Gog and Magog (auxiliary)**: If an exclusive geographical location is exegetically required in Ezek 38-39, this auxiliary point falls (but not the core of the system).

### 8.1 The two arguments and their relative weight

The refutation of dispensationalism rests on **two distinct arguments, which must
not be confused because they have different weight and scope**:

| Argument | Nature | What it shows | What happens if it fails |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Biblical** (README §3.2): the Church is the Israel of God (Gal 6:16), the olive tree is one (Rom 11:17-24), the temple has ceased (Mt 24:1-2; Jn 2:19-21) | **Nuclear.** Exegesis of the text | That the Israel/Church distinction is false as a *theological category* | If it fails, the whole framework falls (cf. §6) |
| **Fruit-based** (README §3.5 and §8.5): what the system has produced in Gaza, Iran, the Middle East and the modern State of Israel with the support of Christian Zionism | **Auxiliary.** Evaluation of consequences | That the hypothesis, besides being unfounded, **produces fruits of death** (Mt 7:16-20) | If the reader disputes the reading of the facts, the biblical argument **remains intact** |

**Why they are separated.** The fruit-based argument is historically
circumstantial: the concrete facts can be disputed, dated otherwise or
attributed to political causes unrelated to theology. The biblical argument does
not depend on any contemporary event: it stands or falls with the text.
Therefore the framework **does not rest its thesis on the fruits**, but uses them
as confirmation and as an ethical warning: a theology that leads to dehumanising
brothers in the faith cannot come from the wisdom from above (Jas 3:17; cf.
§8.5).

**How to read it.** Scripture says that theology is not neutral and is known by
its fruits (Mt 7:16-20; Jas 3:17). But the order of the argument is the reverse
of the one sometimes attributed to us: **first the text, then the fruits**.
Whoever refutes our reading of the facts will not have refuted the thesis;
whoever refutes the biblical argument will have refuted it, and then the fruits
cease to matter for the judgment of the system.

### 8.2 The distinction is not a Christian invention: it also exists among Jews

This document does not hold that Israel should lack a land nor that the Jewish
people have no right to live in it. It holds something more limited: that **the
modern State of Israel is not the Israel of God**, and that this distinction **is
not a Christian invention**.

Among Jews themselves there are those who reject the equation between the State
and the covenant people. It occurs in several directions:

| Current | Its rejection |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Anti-Zionist ultra-Orthodox Judaism** (Neturei Karta, Satmar and others) | The State is a usurpation of the Messiah: the return and sovereignty must come from God, not from a human assembly. Some go as far as opposing the existence of the State as a sovereign entity. |
| **Reform and Reconstructionist Judaism** | The Jewish vocation is religious and ethical, not the sovereignty of a State; the land is a symbol of hope, not a title of state property. |
| **Critical Zionist thinkers** (e.g. Martin Buber) | A binational Zionism: two peoples in the same land with equal rights, without exclusivity of one people over the other. |
| **Secular and left-wing Jews** | They reject the definition of the State as a "Jewish State" because of its consequences for citizenship and the rights of non-Jews. |

**Why this matters to the argument.** If the identification "modern State =
Israel of God" were evident, it would be accepted by those who know the Hebrew
text best. It is not so: there are Jews who reject it for theological reasons,
and there are Jews who reject it for ethical and political reasons. The Christian
argument of this document coincides with them on one point and only one: **the
State is not the covenant people**.

**What this document does not claim.** It is not held that Israel should not
exist, nor that Jews have no right to the land, nor that they should be expelled.
The opposite of what is sometimes attributed to us is held: **the same measure
for everyone**. Israel as a State has the right to exist in security;
Palestinians have the right to the same security, the same land and the same
rights. The critique of Christian Zionism is not a critique of the Jewish people:
it is a critique of those who assign to the State a divine function that the
people itself, from different currents, questions.

### 8.3 The empirical impossibility of "ethnic Israel"

Dispensationalism needs "ethnic Israel" to be identifiable as biological
descendants of Jacob. Three arguments show that the category is inoperable.

#### 8.3.1 The genetic argument

The Israeli geneticist Eran Elhaik (University of Haifa) states: *"There is no
Jewish genome and, of course, there is no Jewish gene"*.

An estimate published in 2025 suggests that up to 152 million people (1.85% of
the world population) carry traces of Jewish ancestry in their DNA. If one tried
to define a threshold of "Jewish DNA", at 100% there would be no individuals and
at 0.1% there would be millions of people worldwide who do not consider
themselves Jews.

**The category is inoperable:** there is no definable genetic threshold that
coincides with belonging to the people of God.

#### 8.3.2 The matrilineal argument

If dispensationalism used the traditional halakhic criterion of a "Jewish mother"
to define "ethnic Israel", **it would exclude David and Jesus**:

- **Ruth** was a Moabite (Ruth 1:4, 16-17) and is in David's direct maternal line (Ruth 4:13-17).
- **Rahab** was a Canaanite (Josh 2:1; 6:25) and is in the genealogy of Boaz and of Jesus (Mt 1:5).
- If the criterion is a "Jewish mother", David does not qualify, and neither does Jesus by his legal genealogy.
- If the criterion is patrilineal, Rahab and Ruth do not qualify.

**There is no single ethnic criterion that includes David, Jesus, Rahab and
Ruth.** Scripture defines the people of God by **faith**, not by genealogy
(Josh 2; Ruth 1; Num 32:12; Ex 12:38).

#### 8.3.3 The political-legal argument

In practice, the modern State of Israel offers no verifiable criterion of
"biological descent from Jacob":

- The **Law of Return** (1950, amended in 1970) grants citizenship to Jews (born of a Jewish mother or converted) and to their children, grandchildren and spouses, based on **legal documentation**, not on genetics.
- In addition, ~1.7 million Palestinians hold Israeli citizenship by historical residence (those who remained in 1948), not by Jewish ancestry.
- None of these criteria is "biological descent from Jacob".

**Dispensationalism ends up operating with a political-legal criterion, neither
genetic nor of faith.**

### 8.4 Old Testament wars do not authorise modern wars

It is common to justify military operations of the modern State of Israel with
the wars of the conquest or with the campaigns of Saul and David. The text does
not allow that transfer, for five reasons.

**1. Every Old Testament war had an explicit prophetic command.** There was no
holy war without a direct mandate from God to a recognised prophet: Joshua
(Josh 1:1-9), Samuel to Saul (1 Sam 15:1-3), Gad and Nathan to David
(2 Sam 7:1-17). Saul was **rejected** precisely for not carrying out the command
(1 Sam 15:22-23). The criterion was not "we are the people", but "God commanded
it through his prophet".

**2. God Himself fought, with signs no one could claim.** The Red Sea
(Ex 14:21-22), the Jordan (Josh 3:14-17), the sun standing still
(Josh 10:12-14), the thunder over the Philistines (1 Sam 7:10). They were direct
operations of God, not merely military victories interpreted as miracle.

**3. The scope was limited and the motive declared.** The conquest wars were
limited to the nations of Canaan because of the fullness of their wickedness
(Gen 15:16; Lev 18:24-28; Deut 9:4-5; Deut 20:16-18). It was not a desire for
blood on God's part, but accumulated judgment, and not because of Israel's
righteousness ("not for your righteousness", Deut 9:4-5).

**4. The historical purpose has already been fulfilled in Christ.** To preserve
the people who would carry the written Law (Rom 3:2) and would be the matrix of
the Messiah (Rom 9:4-5; Gal 3:24). Once that is fulfilled, no pending function
remains that would require a new territorial people.

**5. After Jesus the prophetic criterion changes and force is no longer the
means.** There is no prophecy in the New Testament that commands war:

| Text | What it establishes |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Mt 26:52** | "All who take the sword will perish by the sword" — said in His own defence |
| **Jn 18:36** | "My kingdom is not of this world; if it were, my servants would fight" — the kingdom is not defended by force |
| **2 Cor 10:4** | "The weapons of our warfare are not carnal" |
| **Mt 5:21-22** | The sixth commandment is deepened to anger and insult |
| **Heb 1:1-2** | "In these last days he has spoken to us by his Son" — the normative revelation is Christ |
| **1 Jn 4:1-3** | The criterion for every spirit or prophet: confessing Jesus come in the flesh |

**Conclusion.** "You shall not murder" (Ex 20:13) remains in force. The Old
Testament exceptions were punctual, verifiable and fulfilled mandates. Jesus not
only did not extend them: he made them more improbable by condemning anger
itself (Mt 5:22) and by denying the use of the sword even in his own defence
(Mt 26:52). The new covenant does not require force to carry the gospel: the
testimony is given in freedom and love, as He gave and commanded it
(Jn 13:34-35; Mt 28:19-20).

**Theological inference:** anyone invoking the conquest to justify modern
military operations must show (a) a recognised prophet with an explicit command,
(b) a verifiable accompanying sign, (c) a scope and motive declared by God.
Without those three, the transfer has no biblical basis. And after Christ,
criterion (a) is moreover subject to 1 Jn 4:1-3.

### 8.5 The Christians of the Holy Land: the missing link

All of the above could be discussed as abstract theology. It stops being so when
one considers who the affected people are: **among the inhabitants of Gaza, the
West Bank, Lebanon and Iran there are Christians**, brothers in the faith. They
are not an irrelevant minority:

| Place | Christian presence |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lebanon** | The country with the highest proportion of Christians in the region; the president, by the national pact, is always a Maronite Christian, and there are Maronite, Greek Orthodox, Melkite, Armenian, Syriac and Protestant communities |
| **West Bank** | Communities in Bethlehem, Beit Sahour, Beit Jala, Ramallah, Jerusalem; among them the Christmas Lutheran Church, whose pastor is Munther Isaac |
| **Gaza** | One of the oldest Christian communities in the world, today reduced and displaced |
| **Iran** | Armenian and Assyrian minorities recognised constitutionally, with reserved seats in parliament; in addition, **underground churches** of believers of Muslim background, who meet in homes and face a real risk of detention |

When a divine judgment is invoked over one of these regions, one is speaking of
**brothers in the faith**, not of an abstract geopolitical category. And in the
case of Iran, the situation aggravates the problem: a war over that country also
reaches believers who already live under persecution, for whom the visible church
does not exist and the temple is a borrowed house.

It is not a question of one nation being more Christian than another, nor of
justifying violence because of the religious composition of a country. It is the
opposite: **the presence of brothers does not make a people a legitimate target,
and their dehumanisation does not become acceptable because it fits a prophetic
scheme.**

The Lutheran pastor **Munther Isaac** (Christmas Lutheran Church, Bethlehem;
academic dean of Bethlehem Bible College; director of the Christ at the Checkpoint
conferences) formulates it thus: Palestinian Christians are "the missing element"
of the story; many Western evangelicals "are surprised to discover that
Palestinian Christians exist". And he points out the practical consequence:
relating to people "as objects of eschatological fantasies, not as neighbours".

Isaac also describes the "four assumptions" of Christian Zionism, which he says
he takes from official statements of churches and leaders:

1. The Abrahamic covenant continues with the present Jewish people and, by association, with the State of Israel.
2. The creation of the State of Israel (1948) is a "sign of God's faithfulness to the Jewish people" or the fulfilment of prophecy.
3. Genesis 12:3 ("I will bless those who bless you") applied to a **secular State**: whoever blesses Israel will be blessed. Isaac calls it "religious manipulation 101 for political ends".
4. The land belongs to the Jewish people as an eternal possession and by divine right.

**What this shows:** when it is said that Gaza is like Ai, it is being asserted,
whether one wants it or not, that God ordered the destruction of people among
whom there are brothers. That contradicts:

| Text | Mandate |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gal 6:10** | "Let us do good to all, and especially to those of the household of faith" |
| **1 Jn 4:20** | "Whoever does not love his brother whom he has seen, how can he love God whom he has not seen?" |
| **1 Cor 12:26** | "If one member suffers, all the members suffer with it" |
| **Jn 13:34-35** | "By this all will know that you are my disciples, if you love one another" |
| **Mt 25:40** | "Inasmuch as you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me" |

**The test of the tree:** a theological system that leads to dehumanising
brothers in the faith cannot come from the wisdom from above, because "the wisdom
from above is first pure, then peaceable, gentle, willing to yield, full of
mercy and good fruits, without uncertainty or hypocrisy" (Jas 3:17).

**Historical origins.** Isaac places the origin of Christian Zionism in
Puritanism, the Balfour Declaration and, above all, **John Nelson Darby**, whose
dispensationalism became very influential in North America through the Scofield
Reference Bible. He distinguishes the European variant (Protestant and Catholic),
based on God's faithfulness to the Jews without eschatological emphasis, from the
American evangelical one, centred on the "last times". As an example of the
first he cites the official declaration of the Protestant Church in the
Netherlands: "the creation of the State of Israel is a sign of God's faithfulness
to the Jewish people".

**Documents of the local Christians.** *Kairos Palestine* (2009) warns that "any
use of the Bible to legitimise political options and positions based on injustice
[...] transforms religion into human ideology and strips the Word of God of its
holiness", and affirms: "our future and theirs are one".

### 8.7 God's desire for all humanity and the desert as a type

Scripture affirms that God's desire is that **all** men be saved and come to the
knowledge of the truth (1 Timothy 2:4; 2 Peter 3:9; Ezekiel 33:11). That desire
is not limited to a group, but embraces all humanity (Genesis 12:3; Isaiah 49:6;
Matthew 28:19; Acts 1:8).

The **Israel in the desert** is the **type** of this reality: God wanted **all**
those who came out of Egypt to enter the promised land, but **only those who had
faith and accepted entered** (Numbers 13:30-33; 14:1-10; 14:20-24;
Joshua 14:6-14). The unbelieving generation remained in the desert
(Numbers 14:29-30; Hebrews 3:16-19), and only Caleb and Joshua, who believed,
entered (Numbers 32:12; Deuteronomy 1:36).

**What counts is not any kind of division** that even God Himself may have
produced to keep His word alive among men, but **faith**. The division among the
Israelites in the desert, Northern Israel and Southern Israel (1 Kings 12), the
division between Jews and Gentiles (Ephesians 2:14-16), the division between the
modern nation of Israel and the Church (Galatians 6:16) — none of those divisions
is the criterion for belonging to the people of God. **The criterion is faith**,
and that faith is what makes God's universal desire be fulfilled in those who
believe.
