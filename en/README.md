## 1. Introduction and executive summary

### 1.1 Purpose of the document

This document presents a **premillennial pretribulational** eschatological
framework that affirms **one single people of God** defined by faith in Christ.
Its central thesis is that the dispensationalist distinction between the
**Church** and the **Jewish nation as a permanent subject of the divine plan** is
false: the Church is the Israel of God (Gal 6:16), the olive tree is one
(Rom 11:17-24), the physical temple has ceased (Mt 24:1-2; Jn 2:19-21), and the
millennium is Christ's reign over all nations, not an era of dominion by the
modern State of Israel.

### 1.2 Central thesis

1. **One single people of God**: Jews and Gentiles united by faith in Christ (Gal 3:28-29; Eph 2:14-16).
2. **The name "Israel" is relational, not genealogical**: it is given through wrestling and pleading with God (Gen 32:28; Hos 12:4), not through the bloodline.
3. **The physical temple has ceased**: Christ is the temple (Jn 2:19-21), and the body of the believer and the church are the temple (1 Cor 3:16; Eph 2:19-22).
4. **The millennium is Christ's reign over the nations**, not a restoration of the temple and the Mosaic law.

### 1.3 What is kept and what is rejected from dispensationalism

We acknowledge the dispensationalist effort to produce a **literal
eschatological interpretation**, including Revelation (a literal 1000-year
millennium, a 7-year tribulation, Daniel 9:27 as a week of years, the second
coming at the end of the tribulation), and we imitate it in this article. But we
reject **the interpretive hypothesis** that Israel and the Church are two peoples
with two distinct plans.

#### 1.3.1 The order of the arguments

The refutation uses **two arguments of different weight**: the **biblical** one
(§3.2) is nuclear (if it falls, the framework falls); the **fruit-based** one
(§3.5) is auxiliary (if the reader disputes our reading of the facts, the
biblical argument remains intact). We apply the reverse of the order sometimes
attributed to us: **first the text, then the fruits**. Full development in §8.1
of the expanded document.

| **Kept (literalist method)** | **Rejected (interpretive hypothesis)** |
| :--- | :--- |
| Pretribulational rapture | Separation of Israel/Church as two peoples with two plans |
| 7-year tribulation | Theological Zionism |
| Literal 1000-year millennium | Spiritual superiority of the modern State of Israel |
| Daniel 9:27 as a week of years | The claim that Old Testament saints are resurrected at the start of the millennium |
| Second coming at the end of the tribulation | Restoration of Levitical sacrifices in the millennium |

Other interpretive hypotheses (such as the identification of Gog and Magog or of
the 144,000) are treated in a work-in-progress document of the fuller
eschatological proposal, with labelled inferences and open tensions
[`Literalist-Eschatological-Framework-expanded.md`](Literalist-Eschatological-Framework-expanded.md).

### 1.4 A note of epistemic humility

This document does not claim that its interpretation is the only possible one,
nor the "correct" one in an absolute sense. It claims that it **appears
consistent** with what we have been able to analyse in detail in Scripture, and
that it is open to revision. Absolute truth belongs to God, and we see "through
a glass, darkly" (1 Corinthians 13:12). The Holy Spirit is the one who guides
into all truth (John 16:13), and to Him we pray in the name of Jesus to illumine
what we still do not understand.

## 2. Israel is another name for the people of God

### 2.1 Biblical foundation and implications of the name Israel

The name "Israel" is not an inherited ethnic surname: it is a **title conferred
by God** on the one who wrestles and prevails while clinging to Him.

| Text | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Gen 32:24-28** | Jacob wrestles with the angel until dawn. The angel says: "Your name shall no longer be Jacob, but Israel; for you have struggled with God and with men, and have prevailed" (v.28). |
| **Gen 35:10** | God confirms the name: "Your name shall no longer be Jacob, but Israel". It is a divine assignment, not a self-naming. |
| **Hos 12:3-4** | "In the womb he took his brother by the heel, and in his strength he prevailed with God. He prevailed with the angel and overcame; he wept and pleaded with him". Hosea interprets the name as victory through dependence and pleading, not through his own strength. |
| **Isa 48:1-2** | "Hear this, house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel... for they name themselves after the holy city". The prophet distinguishes the faithful Israel from the unbelieving Israel that has only the name. |

**Implications**:
1. The name is **relational**, not ethnic. It is given through the relationship with God (wrestling, pleading, prevailing), not through the genealogical chain or an earthly citizenship.
2. Jacob's descendants inherit the name, but **they do not inherit the promise without faith**: Jacob's children who do not believe are "children of Israel" by name, but they are not the "Israel of God" (Rom 9:6-8).
3. The "Israel of God" (Gal 6:16) is the one who has passed from Jacob to Israel, Jew or Gentile. The promise to Jacob is fulfilled in Christ (2 Cor 1:20), not in a State.

### 2.2 Incorporation by faith in the Old Testament

Belonging to the people of God was always defined by **faith** and perseverance
in divine revelation:

| Person / Group | Text | How they enter the people of God |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Circumcised foreigners** | Gen 17:12-13, 23 | By Abraham's decision, not their own; they are a type of God's desire to include all the families of the earth (Gen 12:3; Isa 56:3-8) |
| **Mixed multitude** | Ex 12:38 | Leaves Egypt with Israel; joins by faith the living God who saves |
| **Resident foreigner** | Ex 12:48-49; Num 15:15-16; Lev 19:33-34 | Once circumcised, is "as one born in the land"; one and the same law for native and foreigner |
| **Rahab** | Josh 2; 6:25; Mt 1:5; Heb 11:31 | Canaanite and prostitute; believes in the God of Israel and is incorporated; she is in the genealogy of Jesus |
| **Caleb** | Num 32:12; Josh 14:6-14 | Kenizzite (a foreigner); follows God with his whole heart and enters the promised land |
| **Ruth** | Ruth 1:16-17; 4:13-17; Mt 1:5 | Moabite; the law excluded her (Deut 23:3), but God's grace receives her by faith; great-grandmother of David |

### 2.3 The "Northern Israel" kept the name but was not the Israel of God

| Text | Content |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1 Kings 12:26-33** | Jeroboam sets up golden calves in Bethel and Dan, and appoints priests who were not Levites. The Northern kingdom keeps the name "Israel" but abandons the faith. |
| **Hos 12:3-4** | Hosea recalls that the name "Israel" is given through wrestling and pleading with God, not through genealogy. The Northern kingdom has the name but not the faith. |
| **Isa 48:1-2** | "Hear this, house of Jacob, who are called by the name of Israel... for they name themselves after the holy city". The prophet distinguishes between having the name and having the faith. |

**Application**: The modern nation of Israel has the name "Israel", but that does
not make it the Israel of God. The Israel of God is the one who has passed from
Jacob to Israel by faith, Jew or Gentile (Gal 6:16).

#### 2.3.1 The argument is not ours: it also exists among Jews

The distinction "modern State ≠ Israel of God" **is not a Christian invention**:
it is also rejected by Jewish currents (anti-Zionist ultra-Orthodox, Reform,
critical Zionists such as Buber, and secular Jews), each for its own reasons.
This document does not deny Israel's right to exist, nor the right of Jews to
live in the land: it asks for **the same measure for everyone**. Full
development in §8.2 of the expanded document.

### 2.4 The cessation of the temple as the centre of the covenant

| Text | Content |

**Implication**: The physical temple ceased as the centre of the covenant with

### 2.6 Falsification criterion for this chapter

If it is shown that belonging to Israel is defined by pure genealogy in the Old
Testament, or that the Church is not the Israel of God (Gal 6:16), or that the
olive tree is not one (Rom 11:17-24), this chapter falls.

## 3. Refutation of dispensationalism

### 3.1 The essence of the system according to Ryrie

Charles C. Ryrie, in *Dispensationalism* (Moody Press, 1995), set out the three
elements that, according to him, define a dispensationalist:

1. **Distinction between Israel and the Church**: they are not the same people nor the same plan. The Church does not replace Israel in God's economy.
2. **Consistent literal (grammatical-historical) interpretation** of all Scripture: the prophecies about Israel are fulfilled literally for ethnic and national Israel.
3. **Doxological purpose of biblical history**: God's ultimate end in history is the manifestation of His own glory.

Of these three, the first is the **defining criterion** (*sine qua non*):
without the Israel/Church distinction, the system collapses.

### 3.2 Biblical refutation: one single people

| Dispensationalist claim | Refutation from the one people |
| :--- | :--- |
| The Church and ethnic Israel are two distinct peoples with two distinct plans. | The Church is the Israel of God (Gal 6:16). There are not two peoples. |
| The Church is not the "Israel of God" but a "parenthesis" in God's plan for Israel. | The olive tree is one (Rom 11:17-24). The natural branches were cut off for unbelief and can be grafted back in by faith. |
| The Old Testament promises to Israel are fulfilled in a future ethnic Israel, not in the Church. | "All Israel will be saved" (Rom 11:26) = the fullness of the Israel of God, the body of Israelites who believe in Jesus, incorporated into the one people. |
| There will be a third physical temple in the millennium, with the restoration of sacrifices. | The temple has ceased (Mt 24:1-2; Jn 2:19-21). Christ is the temple (Rev 21:22). |
| The millennium is an era of Jewish ethnic dominion with the restoration of the temple and the law. | The millennium is Christ's reign over all nations (Rev 20:4-6; Isa 65:20-25). |

**Without this distinction, the whole system falls:** there are no two plans, no
parenthesis, no third temple, no ethnic millennium. Refuting this distinction is
attacking the foundation of dispensationalism, not one of its branches.

**What is refuted is not a non-existent ethnicity**, but the **superiority** that
Zionism attributes to the modern State of Israel.

### 3.3 Empirical refutation: the impossibility of "ethnic Israel"

Dispensationalism needs "ethnic Israel" to be identifiable as biological
descendants of Jacob. Three arguments show that the category is inoperable:

1. **Genetic**: there is no definable "Jewish DNA" threshold (Elhaik: "there is no Jewish genome").
2. **Matrilineal**: the halakhic criterion of a "Jewish mother" **would exclude David and Jesus**, because Ruth was a Moabite and Rahab a Canaanite (Ruth 4:13-17; Mt 1:5).
3. **Political-legal**: the Law of Return rests on documentation, not genetics; and ~1.7 million Palestinians hold Israeli citizenship by historical residence.

Dispensationalism ends up operating with a political-legal criterion, neither
genetic nor of faith. Full development in §8.3 of the expanded document.

### 3.4 Theological refutation: Old Testament wars do not authorise modern wars

It is common to justify military operations of the modern State of Israel with
the wars of the conquest. The text does not allow that transfer, for five
reasons: (1) every Old Testament war had an **explicit prophetic command**
(Josh 1:1-9; 1 Sam 15:1-3); (2) God Himself fought with **signs no one could
claim** (Ex 14:21-22; Josh 10:12-14); (3) the **scope and motive** were declared
and limited to Canaan because of its wickedness (Gen 15:16; Deut 9:4-5); (4) the
**historical purpose has already been fulfilled in Christ** (Rom 3:2; Gal 3:24);
and (5) **after Jesus there is no prophecy that commands war**: "all who take
the sword will perish by the sword" (Mt 26:52), "my kingdom is not of this
world" (Jn 18:36), "the weapons of our warfare are not carnal" (2 Cor 10:4).

**Resulting criterion:** anyone invoking the conquest to justify modern
operations must show (a) a recognised prophet with an explicit command, (b) a
verifiable accompanying sign, and (c) a scope and motive declared by God;
moreover, every spirit must confess Jesus come in the flesh (1 Jn 4:1-3). Full
development in §8.4 of the expanded document.

### 3.5 Practical consequences of the system

Dispensationalism is not only an eschatological hypothesis: it has verifiable
ethical consequences.

#### 3.5.1 Christian Zionism: Calvary Chapel and Chuck Smith

**a) The separation of peoples as a confessed premise.** In *The Calvary Chapel Distinctives*, Chuck Smith formulates the core of the system clearly:

> "The reason some people get their prophetic scenarios confused is because they spiritualize and make the church Israel. They say, 'God is through with the nation of Israel because they rejected the Messiah.' They believe that God has discarded Israel and replaced it with the church, and the church is now 'the Israel of God.' [...] When you do that, it confuses the whole prophetic picture!"

And he adds: "The church is the elect, but Israel is also the elect", to conclude:
"If the sun came up this morning, then God's covenant with Israel still stands
[...] God is not through with Israel".

**What this shows:** the separation of peoples is not a secondary detail; it is
the premise that sustains the whole prophetic system. Removing it does not
"confuse the prophetic picture": it simplifies it. There is one single people,
and its criterion is faith (Gal 6:16; Rom 9:6-8).

**b) Chronology as the engine of Zionism.** In *End Times: A Report on Future Survival* (1978), p. 35, Smith writes:

> "Since a generation of judgment is forty years and the Tribulation period lasts seven years, I believe the Lord could come back for His Church any time before the Tribulation starts, which would mean any time before 1981. (1948 + 40 − 7 = 1981)."

The State of 1948 was not a peripheral datum: it was **the premise of the
calculation**. If Israel does not fulfil the fig tree of Mt 24:32-34, the scheme
collapses. That is the structural reason for such a marked Zionism: the
eschatology depends on the modern State being a permanent subject of the divine
plan.

#### 3.5.2 The erosion of pacifist ethics: the Canadian Mennonite case

Ken Bechtel, "A Premillennialist Pacifism: The Canadian Swiss Mennonite Peace
Position", *Journal of Mennonite Studies* 25 (2007), 89-106, documents that Swiss
Mennonites in Ontario adopted dispensationalist premillennialism during the
Second World War and that this theology, in some cases, introduced a
"rationalisation and justification of violence by the State", eroding their
traditional pacifist ethics.

**The mechanism is always the same:** if the Sermon on the Mount belongs to the
future millennium and not to this age, an autonomous space remains where state
violence is not judged by the ethics of Jesus. It comes from within the Mennonite
tradition itself, which makes it especially significant for us.

**This touches us closely.** We are not speaking of someone else's error. The
case documented by Bechtel did not occur among liberals or unbelievers: it
occurred among Mennonites who wanted to be faithful to Scripture and to preserve
their pacifist heritage. The system did not ask them to renounce the faith; it
only asked them to assign the Sermon on the Mount to another age. The rest came
on its own.

#### 3.5.3 The Christians of the Holy Land: the missing link

All of the above could be discussed as abstract theology. It stops being so when
one considers who the affected people are: **among the inhabitants of Gaza, the
West Bank, Lebanon and Iran there are Christians**, brothers in the faith.

| Place | Christian presence |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Lebanon** | The country with the highest proportion of Christians in the region; the president, by the national pact, is always a Maronite Christian |
| **West Bank** | Communities in Bethlehem, Beit Sahour, Beit Jala, Ramallah, Jerusalem (among them the Christmas Lutheran Church, whose pastor is Munther Isaac) |
| **Gaza** | One of the oldest Christian communities in the world, today reduced and displaced |
| **Iran** | Recognised Armenian and Assyrian minorities, and **underground churches** of believers of Muslim background, at real risk of detention |

When a divine judgment is invoked over one of these regions, one is speaking of
**brothers in the faith**, not of an abstract geopolitical category. **The
presence of brothers does not make a people a legitimate target, and their
dehumanisation does not become acceptable because it fits a prophetic scheme.**

**The test of the tree:** a theological system that leads to dehumanising
brothers in the faith cannot come from the wisdom from above (Jas 3:17; cf.
Gal 6:10; 1 Jn 4:20; 1 Cor 12:26; Mt 25:40).

Isaac, the "four assumptions" of Christian Zionism, the historical origins
(Darby, Scofield and the Balfour Declaration) and *Kairos Palestine* are
developed in §8.5 of the expanded document.

### 3.6 Falsification criterion for this chapter

1. **Church/Israel distinction**: if it is shown that dispensationalism is correct in its Church/ethnic Israel distinction, this chapter falls.
2. **Old Testament wars (§3.4)**: if a New Testament prophecy commanding a war is shown, or a New Testament criterion authorising taking the sword for the kingdom, §3.4 falls.
3. **Ethnic Israel (§3.3)**: if a verifiable genetic, matrilineal or political-legal criterion identifying "ethnic Israel" with the modern State is shown, §3.3 falls.

## 4. Eschatological proposal

The framework keeps the literalist method: premillennialism, a 7-year tribulation
with a pretribulational rapture, a literal 1000-year millennium and Daniel 9:27
as a week of years. What changes is **one hypothesis of that method**: there are
not two peoples with two plans, but one only, defined by faith in Christ.

## 5. Conclusion

**One single people.** The criterion for belonging to the people of God is faith
in Christ, not genealogy nor the sovereignty of a State (Gal 6:16; Rom 9:6-8;
Rom 11:17-24). The Old Testament already showed it: the name "Israel" is
received through wrestling and pleading (Gen 32:28; Hos 12:4), and Rahab, Ruth,
Caleb and the foreigner entered before Christ (§2).

**The dispensationalist premise falls.** If the people is one, there are no two
plans and no parenthesis of the Church, and the literalist method is applied
coherently: pretribulational rapture, a seven-year tribulation, Daniel 9:27 as a
week of years, a 1000-year millennium and the second coming at the end of the
tribulation (§1.3, §4).

**Practical consequence.** The distinction between biblical Israel and the modern
State follows from the thesis, and with it the same measure for everyone: Israel
is not denied its right to exist, but the divine function that Christian Zionism
attributes to it is denied (§2.3.1, §3.5).

**What is not claimed.** This is not the only possible reading. Each chapter
declares its falsification criterion (§2.6, §3.6; expanded §6), and the
fruit-based argument only confirms, it does not sustain (§1.3.1). The framework
is offered for examination by Scripture and to the guidance of the Holy Spirit
(Jn 16:13). Its full development is in §4.7 of the expanded document.
